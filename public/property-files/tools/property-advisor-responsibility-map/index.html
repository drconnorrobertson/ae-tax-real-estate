Build a reviewable record

Agree who supplies, reviews and implements the property analysis.

Define the reporting period and affected owner, entity or property before entering records. For each item, use a source reference that another person can retrieve. Mark assumptions explicitly and assign unresolved items to the person who can supply evidence.

Use the register in a review meeting

Begin with items marked missing or needs review. Confirm whether each affects the decision or merely its timing. Record the question requiring professional judgment rather than selecting a tax outcome from the completion count. Export the register and keep it with the engagement scope and final reviewer response.

Close the loop

After the reviewer responds, retain the source document, written conclusion and implementation responsibility together. Reopen the file if ownership, reporting period, expected transaction or underlying records change.

Choose the related context

Single-family long-term rentals

Keep the purchase, tenant use and subsequent improvements linked to the same property identifier.

Duplexes

Separate unit-specific work from systems serving both units.

Triplexes

Map the three units and common-area costs before assigning figures to an individual unit.

Fourplexes

Reconcile apartment, common-area and exterior work to the total project.

Small apartment buildings

Preserve unit counts, shared amenities and each capital project's boundaries.

Large apartment communities

Use stable property and building identifiers throughout acquisition and project records.

Condominium rentals

Identify unit ownership separately from association-owned facilities and expenditures.

Townhouse rentals

Distinguish unit-specific construction from shared facilities governed by agreements.

Short-term rental houses

Preserve booking history, operating tasks and separately purchased guest furnishings.

Vacation cabins

Explain site work, furnishing purchases and seasonal availability.

Beach rentals

Record weather-related repairs, later improvements and personal-use periods separately.

Mountain rentals

Explain seasonal operation, access work and phased projects.

Mixed-use buildings

Map commercial and residential areas and shared systems.

Owner-occupied office buildings

Separate building ownership, operating-company use and any third-party tenants.

Medical office buildings

Explain tenant equipment, specialized installations and general building systems.

Dental office buildings

Distinguish dental equipment and related work from general facility construction.

Retail buildings

Separate landlord improvements, tenant work and common facilities.

Restaurant buildings

Document kitchen equipment, restaurant installations and building ownership.

Warehouses

Distinguish storage equipment, tenant installations and building systems.

Self-storage properties

Map storage buildings, access systems, paved areas and later additions.

Manufacturing properties

Describe machinery-related work and general plant construction separately.

Rental portfolios

Maintain a property-by-property map before aggregating results.

Real Estate Tax FAQ

What is a cost segregation study?

A cost segregation study analyzes property components and their appropriate depreciation treatment. A study is not automatically approved by the IRS. Review the methodology, asset classifications and supporting evidence. See the IRS Cost Segregation Audit Technique Guide.

Can I do a cost seg on a property I have owned for years?

An existing property can be reviewed. Whether a correction uses an amended return or an accounting-method change depends on the prior reporting and applicable procedure. Ask the preparer to determine the proper route and any owner-level loss limits.

How does the STR tax strategy work?

An average customer-use period of seven days or less generally falls outside the rental-activity definition under the passive activity rules. That alone does not make the activity nonpassive. More than 100 hours is one participation test only when the taxpayer also participates at least as much as any other individual. Other tests and loss limits must be reviewed. See IRS Publication 925.

Is bonus depreciation still available?

Current federal guidance provides 100% bonus depreciation for qualifying property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025. Eligibility, elections, acquisition rules and state treatment require review. The building itself and every study component do not automatically qualify. See IRS depreciation guidance.

How much does a cost segregation study cost?

AE publishes $1 per square foot with a $2,000 minimum. Confirm the current fee, property scope and separately priced implementation work in a written proposal. A quote does not guarantee tax savings. Review AE study pricing.

Do I need Real Estate Professional status?

Not every property owner needs real estate professional status. For an activity outside the rental-activity definition, material participation may be relevant without that status. Eligibility and the usability of any loss require a complete facts-based review. See IRS Publication 925.

Do Guest Extensions Change the Seven-Day Short-Term Rental Average?

Yes. For the federal passive activity rules, average customer use depends on periods of customer use and their length. If a guest extends a continuous stay, a platform may report two transactions where the records support one longer customer-use period.

How the extension changes the calculation

Suppose Guest A reserved four days and added three more without a break in the right to use the unit. Guest B reserved eight days. If those were the only two customer-use periods for that property, the calculation would be 15 days divided by two periods, or 7.5 days. Counting three platform transaction rows would incorrectly show an average of five days.

In this example, the property would fall outside the seven-days-or-less exception on these facts. Review whether another exception applies before deciding how to classify the activity.

How to reconcile the records

  1. Export the original reservation and keep any extensions, cancellations and refund adjustments.
  2. Compare the contract dates with the right-to-use dates. Document any gap, unit switch or separate reservation.
  3. Assign a common stay identifier to transaction rows that belong to the same use period. Retain the original transaction identifiers so the grouping can be reproduced.
  4. Total the supported days and divide by the supported periods. Keep separate records for the owners' work and any participation by other people.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I count a continuous stay extension as a separate booking?

Group the records according to the guest's continuous right to use the rental. If the guest retained that right through the extension, the separate transaction can be part of the same customer-use period.

Does an unbooked night count toward the average?

A vacant night between customer-use periods does not enter the calculation. If the guest still held the right to use the property that night, review the booking terms and activity records before excluding it.

Does an average of seven days make the loss deductible against wages?

Seven days or less can satisfy one exception to the passive activity rules' definition of rental activity. Whether a loss can offset wages also depends on material participation and other applicable loss limits.

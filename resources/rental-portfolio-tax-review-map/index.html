Map Your Rental Portfolio Before a Tax Review

A rental portfolio review should begin with a map of properties, owners, use, and reporting history. That inventory helps the advisor spot missing schedules and distinguish a property-specific issue from a portfolio-wide assumption.

Identify owners and entities

List the legal owner, ownership percentage, entity tax classification, and property address. Keep ownership documents with each property. Similar LLC names do not establish that properties have the same tax reporting or ownership structure.

Document actual use

Record long-term leases, short-term stays, personal use, vacancies, and changes during the year. Summarize the dates and keep supporting calendars. The description investment property is not enough to resolve every reporting question.

Reconcile cumulative records

Match each property to acquisition records, depreciation schedules, improvements, and limited-loss carryforwards. Note which preparer last handled the property and which records are missing. A clean annual profit and loss statement does not recreate historical basis.

Prioritize the review

Identify upcoming sales, refinances, major improvements, and unresolved prior-year issues. Give the advisor those events before discussing new deductions. Review passive activity and at-risk treatment based on actual facts rather than assuming every portfolio loss can offset other income.

Action checklist

  • List owners and entities
  • Document use by property
  • Match historical schedules
  • Identify missing records
  • Flag planned transactions

Should every rental in a portfolio use identical tax treatment?

No. Ownership, use, reporting history, and taxpayer circumstances can differ across properties.

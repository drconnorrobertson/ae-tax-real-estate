What Goes Into a Partial Disposition Workpaper for a Replaced Rental Roof?

The price of the new roof does not establish the old roof’s basis. When contemporaneous records are unavailable, use a reasonable method allowed by the regulations and document differences in scope, condition and pricing.

Worked example

Assume contemporaneous invoices and the building asset schedule support $24,000 of unadjusted basis for the disposed roof and $9,000 of depreciation allowed or allowable. That leaves $15,000 of adjusted basis to evaluate under the partial disposition rules before considering any proceeds or other adjustments. A $41,000 replacement contract belongs on a separate schedule. Use the workpaper to check the old component, the new project, and the filing decision independently.

Records to prepare

  1. Identify exactly which sections and layers were removed and preserve contractor photos or scope descriptions.
  2. Tie the retired component’s basis to applicable records and calculate depreciation through disposition.
  3. Record the date the new roof was placed in service and document the filing decision for the year of removal.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use the new roof invoice as the old roof’s basis?

The price of the new roof does not establish the old roof’s basis. When contemporaneous records are unavailable, use a reasonable method allowed by the regulations and document differences in scope, condition and pricing.

What if the old roof has no remaining adjusted basis?

With no remaining adjusted basis, the workpaper should show why there is no basis left to deduct. Reconcile the amount allocated to the old roof and depreciation allowed or allowable.

When do I raise a partial disposition election?

Raise it before filing the timely original federal return for the disposition year, including extensions. Confirm method, eligibility and election timing for the particular facts before filing.

Bridge the rent roll to actual rental receipts

Reconcile scheduled rent, adjustments, tenant payments and bank receipts without treating the rent roll as a cash report.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Property operating guides

A rent roll describes scheduled charges or occupancy information; it does not automatically show what reached the property bank account. Build a bridge from the schedule to recorded tenant activity and then to settlement. This helps an owner explain collection differences without changing the original records to force a match. The resulting schedule supports accounting review rather than deciding how rental income must be reported.

Define the rent-roll snapshot

Record the property, units, covered period and date the rent roll was produced. Identify whether it shows contractual rent, current charges or another measure. A report downloaded later may reflect amendments that were not in place at the review date. Retain the original snapshot and note changes separately so comparisons use a consistent population.

List approved charge changes

Connect concessions, additional charges, reversals and other changes to their supporting records. Distinguish a changed amount from a payment that arrived late. The property manager should resolve uncertain lease or billing details through the appropriate process. An unpaid charge should not become an approved reduction simply because it makes the reconciliation easier.

Match tenant activity to settlement

Link tenant ledger receipts with bank deposits or processor settlements using dates and references. Separate grouped payments, fees, returned payments and amounts not yet settled. For a fictional month with $8,000 of recorded tenant payments and a $7,800 bank settlement, the $200 difference needs an explanation; it is not automatically a missing rent payment.

Explain collection timing

Show opening unpaid balances, current charges, payments applied and closing balances using the system’s definitions. Note payments covering another period and funds applied to something other than rent. Do not infer cash availability from a receipt that was reversed later. Keep collection assumptions in the forecast separate from verified payments in the reconciliation.

Deliver the bridge with source records

Have the property manager confirm tenant-ledger questions and finance confirm settlement differences. Keep the rent-roll snapshot, adjustment approvals, ledger extract and bank evidence together. Ask the preparer to determine any reporting entries from those facts. Preserve the unresolved-item list with an owner and next review date rather than hiding differences in a single balancing entry.

Prepare the review file

  • Dated rent-roll snapshot and definitions
  • Approved charge-change references
  • Tenant-to-bank receipt matching
  • Timing and reversal explanations
  • Signed-off bridge and open items

Prepare property-use and policy questions before an insurance renewal

Gather property-use changes, policy versions and unanswered questions for an insurance professional before renewal.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 21, 2026

Property operating guides

Prepare questions before renewing

A rental insurance renewal is an opportunity to verify the property facts supplied to the insurance professional and identify questions about the actual policy. Use this file to connect use, occupancy and changes at the property to the relevant declaration or endorsement. It does not determine coverage, recommend a policy or promise payment for a loss. The existing insurance-claim restoration guide remains the separate record for a claim that has already occurred.

Describe the property as it is used

Record the rental pattern, any owner use and changes since the last insurance review. Identify renovations, vacant periods or other facts the insurance professional asks about without deciding their effect on coverage. Preserve the source of each description and distinguish an expected future use from current operation. A short description such as rental house may not tell the professional enough about the arrangement. Ask which additional information is needed and keep the response with the renewal file.

Fictional example: a changed rental pattern

A fictional owner previously rented to one long-term tenant and is considering shorter stays after the lease ends. The owner logs the proposed change and sends the relevant facts to the insurance professional before relying on a renewal quotation. The resulting response may request additional details or different documents. The file does not assume that the current policy will cover the new use, that a particular endorsement solves the question or that a higher premium guarantees a specific protection.

Read the contract through qualified advice

The Insurance Information Institute explains that different rental scenarios can require discussion with an insurance professional. That general guidance cannot establish the terms of an individual policy. Keep the declarations, relevant endorsements and the written response to each question together. Distinguish a quote from a policy issued and an illustrative statement from a binding coverage explanation. Ask the insurer or licensed professional to interpret exclusions, conditions and limits rather than copying assumptions from another property in the portfolio.

Leave a clear renewal trail

Identify the policy version accepted, the facts supplied and any unanswered questions requiring follow-up. If the proposed property use changes again, return to the professional instead of reusing advice given on different facts. Keep claim files and renewal files linked when relevant but do not merge them into a single conclusion. The completed packet should let the owner and adviser understand what was asked and answered. It is evidence of the review conversation, not a certification that every potential loss is insured. If several properties share one policy document, identify which location and endorsement each question concerns. A response about one address should not be treated as confirmation of the same terms for every building.

Fields for your working record

Download the blank local CSV

The download contains column headings and one empty row, with no formulas or example personal data. Open it in your spreadsheet application and save it locally. Editing the file does not transmit its contents to this website. Keep original source documents secure and use references in the record.

  • Property and use fact
  • Policy or endorsement reference
  • Change evidence
  • Question to insurance professional
  • Response reference
  • Review date
  • Review status
  • Unresolved question

Track co-owner property funding and reimbursements with a clear trail

Separate contributions, advances, repayments and expense reimbursements before reconciling a jointly owned property.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Property operating guides

When several owners fund a property, bank transfers alone may not show what each payment represents. Maintain a register that connects the amount to the approved purpose and supporting records. The file supports professional review; it does not determine ownership percentages, create a loan, change an agreement or establish anyone’s tax basis.

Start with the parties and governing records

Identify the property, legal owner, participants and relevant agreements. Use consistent names or secure reference identifiers. Distinguish an individual owner from an entity controlled by that person. Ask the appropriate advisers to resolve inconsistencies between the records and the intended arrangement rather than allowing a spreadsheet label to redefine it.

Describe each transfer separately

Record date, amount, sending and receiving accounts, stated purpose and approval reference. Keep proposed contributions separate from settled funds. Do not classify every incoming owner transfer as rent or every outgoing payment as a distribution. Use the available evidence and retain questions for the professional responsible for classification.

Connect expenses and reimbursements

Where an owner pays a property bill directly, link the invoice, payment proof and reimbursement request. For a fictional $900 repair paid by one owner and reimbursed $600 by the property, show both events and the remaining $300 question. The example does not determine who owes the balance or whether the cost is deductible.

Reconcile outstanding amounts

Compare the register with bank records, property books and statements from the relevant parties. Separate agreed amounts from disputed items and unexplained differences. Prevent a direct payment from being counted again as a new contribution when a later reimbursement occurs. Record correction entries with a reason and approver.

Prepare the review and confirmation

Give the accountant the agreements, transfer register and supporting bills together. Ask for written treatment of unresolved advances, contributions or repayments. Have the responsible parties review the factual balances through an appropriate process. Preserve the final reconciliation rather than using a verbal understanding as the only evidence for a later transaction.

Prepare the review file

  • Legal-owner and participant references
  • Transfer purposes and approval evidence
  • Invoices and direct-payment records
  • Reimbursements and unsettled differences
  • Professional classification and factual confirmation

Sources and review scope

These are editorial preparation steps. IRS guidance explains the importance of supporting records and distinguishes types of rental receipts and costs.

Build a rental lease-renewal cash calendar

Separate notice dates, renewal decisions, rent changes and expected collections in a property planning calendar.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Property operating guides

A lease end date does not by itself tell an owner when rent might change or when a tenant will decide to remain. A renewal calendar connects the actual agreement, the management process and the cash forecast. Use it to identify conversations and unresolved assumptions. The calendar does not interpret renewal rights or establish notice requirements; those questions depend on the applicable agreement and rules.

Inventory the current lease terms

List each unit, current term, end date and relevant agreement version. Identify amendments and record where the complete documents are kept. Do not rely solely on a property-system end date if the underlying agreement has changed. Ask the responsible manager or qualified adviser to confirm ambiguous terms before they drive a decision.

Separate actions from outcomes

Record the dates for internal review, permitted communications, expected tenant response and other steps confirmed for the situation. Distinguish an action the owner controls from an outcome that remains uncertain. A proposed renewal is not a signed commitment, and a reminder in a spreadsheet is not proof that a required notice was delivered.

Model rent and timing independently

For a fictional unit currently paying $1,400 per month, a proposed $1,500 renewal changes expected monthly receipts by $100 only if the proposal takes effect as assumed. A gap before a new term may matter more than that increase. Show the rate, start date and collection assumption separately, without presenting the proposal as agreed income.

Include transition costs and alternatives

Identify potential marketing, turnover work and other costs if the tenant leaves, using quotes or clearly labeled estimates. Compare scenarios on the same time horizon. Avoid assuming every vacancy produces the same work or duration. Record which assumptions need a site inspection, provider estimate or management decision before the forecast can be relied on.

Update the calendar with evidence

When a decision is documented, link the signed agreement or other relevant evidence and update the cash forecast. Preserve the earlier scenario for comparison. Assign unresolved items to a named role and date. Ask finance to review how any charges, concessions or other amounts flow into the records instead of treating the calendar itself as an accounting instruction.

Prepare the review file

  • Complete lease and amendment references
  • Confirmed action dates and responsible roles
  • Separate proposal and signed status
  • Rent, vacancy and cost scenarios
  • Evidence of decisions and forecast updates

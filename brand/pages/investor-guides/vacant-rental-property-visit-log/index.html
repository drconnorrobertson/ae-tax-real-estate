Keep a useful vacant-property visit and exception log

Record visit scope, observations, follow-up and responsibility during a rental vacancy without implying a safety inspection.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Property operating guides

A vacant-property visit can mean a basic visual check, a maintenance appointment or another defined service. A log is useful only when it states what was actually done and who will handle the findings. Agree on the visit scope with qualified people for the property. This guide does not specify a safe inspection method or replace insurance, security or maintenance requirements.

Define the agreed visit scope

Record the property, authorized visitor, expected frequency and tasks the arrangement covers. Identify areas that will not be accessed or systems that will not be tested. Confirm any requirements that arise from the actual contract or policy with the appropriate professional. A label such as property check should not imply a comprehensive inspection.

Capture observations with context

Record the date, time, conditions relevant to the visit and the observations made within the agreed scope. Link photographs or reports through an approved system. Separate what the visitor saw from a suspected cause. A damp area, for example, is an observation; the cause may need a qualified assessment.

Assign each follow-up

List the issue, person responsible, priority assigned through the agreed process and next action. For a fictional visit that identifies a damaged window covering, the log can show who will obtain a quote and when the owner will review it. It should not imply that documenting the issue alone resolved the underlying concern.

Connect visits to approved work

Link subsequent work orders, approvals, invoices and completion evidence to the original finding. Keep unresolved items open and explain delays. Use qualified providers for technical or safety assessments. Maintain clear access permissions rather than sharing credentials broadly with anyone asked to help during the vacancy.

Review coverage and closeout

Compare actual visits with the agreed schedule and explain missed or changed visits. At the end of the vacancy, give the responsible manager the open-item list and relevant records. Keep this operational history separate from conclusions about coverage, compliance, tax treatment or the property’s readiness for occupancy.

Prepare the review file

  • Defined visit scope and authorized role
  • Dated observations and supporting records
  • Named follow-up and next action
  • Linked approvals and completed work
  • Schedule reconciliation and vacancy closeout

Review rental cash before making an owner distribution

Connect available cash, scheduled obligations, reserves and approval records without equating a bank balance with distributable profit.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Property operating guides

The bank balance for a rental property may include money needed for upcoming bills, held amounts or other commitments. A distribution review should explain those items before an owner decides how much to withdraw. The review does not determine legal distribution rights, tax consequences or basis. Use the governing arrangements and qualified advice alongside the cash schedule.

Start with reconciled cash

Identify the accounts included, balance date and outstanding transactions. Reconcile the bank figure with the property records before using it as a starting point. A pending deposit or unpresented payment can change the usable amount. Keep business and personal accounts distinct and include transfers in only one place.

List known commitments by date

Record loan payments, approved bills, upcoming work and other obligations with their sources. Identify funds held for another purpose rather than treating all cash as owner money. Confirm restrictions with the relevant agreement or professional. A forecast should distinguish committed payments from optional projects and uncertain scenarios.

Make the working calculation explicit

For a fictional reconciled $20,000 balance with $8,000 in identified near-term commitments and a separately approved $5,000 planning reserve, the remaining arithmetic amount is $7,000. That number is only a discussion starting point. It does not establish permission to distribute or account for every legal, tax or operating consideration.

Record the decision and approval

Identify who can approve a withdrawal and which information was reviewed. Keep any conditions, amounts retained and unresolved questions visible. Do not use the cash worksheet to change ownership allocations or override a governing agreement. Ask the accountant about reporting and basis implications before using a bookkeeping label as a tax conclusion.

Reconcile the actual transfer

Match an approved transfer to its bank record and the relevant owner or entity account. Update the forecast for actual payments and later information. Preserve the pre-decision schedule so the business can understand the reasoning. Revisit the plan when collections, repairs, financing or other commitments change materially.

Prepare the review file

  • Reconciled cash and balance date
  • Dated commitments and held amounts
  • Explicit assumptions and reserve decisions
  • Approval and professional questions
  • Transfer evidence and updated forecast

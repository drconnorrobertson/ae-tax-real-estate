Index move-in condition evidence without deciding damage charges

Connect room-level condition observations with original media, dates and tenant comments without deciding liability or deposit charges.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 21, 2026

Property operating guides

Preserve observations before drawing conclusions

A move-in condition index connects each observed item to its original evidence. It is useful when photographs, notes and tenant comments are stored separately and will need to be understood later. The index does not decide responsibility for damage or authorize a charge against a security deposit. The existing deposit-accounting guide retains that separate workflow. Here the practical goal is a traceable record of what was observed, when and by whom.

Use room and item identifiers

Create a consistent room or area label, then identify the relevant item without collecting unnecessary personal details. Preserve original photograph or video references, the observation date and the observer. If a tenant provides a different description, keep that comment beside the initial record rather than overwriting it. An image without a date or location may still be relevant, but mark the limitation. Do not improve the record by altering an image to remove uncertainty about what it originally showed.

Fictional example: a disputed floor mark

A fictional manager records a floor mark at move-in while the tenant supplies a photograph from an earlier viewing. The index links both images and their dates. At a later review, a second photograph appears to show a different area of the room. The owner records that uncertainty and requests clarification. The file does not label the tenant liable or enter a repair charge simply because the later photograph looks worse. The decision-maker needs the full sequence and applicable rules.

Keep program forms in their proper context

HUD publishes a move-in and move-out inspection form for relevant housing-program use. It illustrates recording conditions and review information, but does not establish the requirements for every rental property. Determine which local rules and program documents apply before selecting forms or deciding what acknowledgments are needed. This guide’s index is not a professional building inspection, a habitability certification or evidence that hidden hazards are absent. Record the scope of the observation and refer technical or legal questions to qualified professionals.

Prepare the comparison packet

Before handing the file over, check that the referenced media can be opened and that the room labels remain consistent. Separate contemporaneous observations from later recollections. Include any requested correction and the response without deleting the first entry. The final packet should show what evidence supports each item and where the record is incomplete. Use the appropriate legal and accounting review for any proposed tenant charge or deposit treatment. Completing the index alone does not settle either question. If a photograph includes personal possessions or identifying information unrelated to the condition review, limit how it is shared. Preserve the original appropriately while supplying reviewers only the evidence they need for the stated purpose.

Fields for your working record

Download the blank local CSV

The download contains column headings and one empty row, with no formulas or example personal data. Open it in your spreadsheet application and save it locally. Editing the file does not transmit its contents to this website. Keep original source documents secure and use references in the record.

  • Unit and room
  • Observation date
  • Original media reference
  • Observer
  • Tenant comment reference
  • Later comparison reference
  • Review status
  • Unresolved question

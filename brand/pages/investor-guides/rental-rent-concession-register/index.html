Track rental rent concessions without losing the original terms

Connect rent concessions, approved changes, tenant charges and actual receipts in a reviewable property record.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Property operating guides

A rent concession changes the amount or timing a tenant is expected to pay, but the property ledger may still show the original schedule. Keep a record of the original terms, the approved change and the resulting charges. The register supports an operational and accounting review; it does not decide enforceability, income recognition or the tax treatment of a particular agreement.

Preserve the original rent schedule

Record the property, unit, lease reference, covered period and original payment schedule. Keep the source agreement available to the authorized reviewer. A monthly total without dates can hide whether the change affects one period or the full lease. Avoid putting unnecessary tenant identifiers in a broadly shared spreadsheet.

Describe the change precisely

Separate a reduced charge, delayed payment, waived fee and other adjustment. Link the written approval and effective dates, and identify who can authorize it. Do not label an unpaid amount a concession merely because collection is uncertain. Questions about what the agreement permits belong with the appropriate property or legal adviser.

Reconcile charges and receipts

Compare the approved schedule with tenant statements, bank or processor receipts and posted adjustments. For a fictional unit with a $1,500 monthly charge and a documented $300 reduction for one month, the revised charge is $1,200 for that month. This arithmetic does not determine tax reporting or change the other months automatically.

Keep timing differences visible

A tenant may pay later than expected, pay several periods together or have an amount applied to a different charge. Record the allocation and explanation. Keep a delayed payment separate from a permanent reduction so the owner can forecast cash using the actual circumstances. Ask the accountant to resolve the reporting consequences using the relevant records.

Close the review with a traceable adjustment

Have the responsible manager confirm the schedule and finance confirm any ledger correction. Preserve the original and revised entries rather than overwriting history. Give the preparer the approved terms, reconciliation and unresolved questions. A clear file should show what changed, why, who approved it and whether the cash was ultimately received.

Prepare the review file

  • Original rent schedule and period
  • Written change and approval reference
  • Revised charge calculation
  • Receipt allocation and timing differences
  • Review owner and ledger correction

Sources and review scope

These are editorial preparation steps. IRS guidance explains the importance of supporting records and distinguishes types of rental receipts and costs.

Review a property manager's promised services against the evidence

Compare a property manager’s agreed services with received reports and task evidence, keeping disputed obligations separate.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 21, 2026

Property operating guides

Review services rather than only transfers

A monthly property-manager statement may reconcile cash without showing whether the other agreed services were delivered. Use this review file to connect a written service commitment to its evidence: a report, inspection summary, communication or completed task. It is separate from the existing statement-reconciliation and manager-transition guides. It does not grade a manager against an invented industry standard or determine that a contract was breached.

Start with the actual agreement

Identify the relevant contract provision and record the service description in its context. Distinguish a fixed reporting commitment from a service available on request or for an additional fee. Record the frequency or trigger as agreed, the evidence expected and the responsible contact. Do not infer an obligation from a sales brochure when the executed agreement says something different. If the parties interpret a provision differently, preserve both explanations and seek appropriate review rather than selecting the interpretation most convenient to the owner.

Fictional example: a missing routine report

A fictional owner expects a recurring property report but receives only financial statements. The owner records the agreement reference, the periods for which a report is expected and the documents received. The manager explains that one report was delayed and another was sent to an old address. The review file distinguishes those responses and tracks the requested copies. It does not automatically deduct fees or label the manager noncompliant; it supplies facts for the owner and any qualified contract reviewer.

Define acceptable evidence carefully

A completed checklist, a dated report and an informal message may support different parts of the promised work. Ask what was actually done, the scope of the observation and any limitations noted. A manager’s routine visit is not necessarily an engineering, environmental or safety inspection. Record that boundary. Keep tenant details and access information out of broad distribution when they are not needed. The objective is an understandable service record, not a larger collection of sensitive data or a substitute for specialist conclusions.

Set the next review action

For each exception, identify the requested information, the contact responsible and the status of the response. Keep service-performance questions separate from financial adjustments until the proper reviewer addresses any contractual consequence. At the next review, confirm that promised follow-up arrived and close only the items supported by evidence. The resulting file can help an owner discuss service expectations or prepare a later transition. It should preserve the distinction between a missing document, an incomplete task and a disputed legal obligation. Agree on the review period before comparing reports. A document issued shortly after month end may relate to the previous period, so use the actual service dates rather than a file-upload date alone.

Fields for your working record

Download the blank local CSV

The download contains column headings and one empty row, with no formulas or example personal data. Open it in your spreadsheet application and save it locally. Editing the file does not transmit its contents to this website. Keep original source documents secure and use references in the record.

  • Frequency as agreed
  • Expected evidence
  • Received report and date
  • Exception reference
  • Manager response
  • Review status

Review a rental leasing commission against the agreed work

Connect the leasing agreement, tenant placement evidence, fee calculation and payment record.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Property operating guides

A leasing invoice may depend on the agreement’s definition of a placement, renewal or other service. Before comparing fees or paying an invoice, assemble the document that sets the terms and the evidence of the work. This review supports a business discussion; it does not determine enforceability, licensing requirements or the legal interpretation of a commission arrangement.

Identify the exact agreement

Record the property, provider, agreement version, service period and approving owner. Identify separate terms for initial leasing, renewals, marketing or management where present. Do not substitute another property’s fee schedule or an old email for the operative terms. Have the responsible professional resolve ambiguity about what triggers a charge.

Describe the work and result

Link marketing records, tenant placement information, signed documents or other evidence relevant to the agreed service. Keep sensitive applicant information in the approved system. A provider’s involvement in a tenancy does not by itself show which fee provision applies. Record the facts needed for the reviewer without assuming the outcome.

Recalculate the stated fee basis

For a fictional agreement expressly setting a fee at half of one month’s $1,600 rent, the calculation is $800. This example is not a market rate or a recommendation. Confirm the actual basis, exclusions and any separate charges before applying arithmetic to an invoice. Keep the calculation and supporting provision together.

Check credits and duplicate charges

Compare the invoice with prior invoices, payments and any management deductions. Identify whether a fee was already withheld from an owner distribution. Separate disputed items from approved amounts and ask for clarification through the agreed channel. A net bank payment can conceal the fee deduction, so include the supporting statement.

Close the file and improve future comparisons

Retain the approved invoice, payment evidence and explanation of adjustments. When selecting a future provider, compare the complete scope and triggers rather than only the headline percentage. Give the accountant the records needed to decide how the actual payment should be reported for the property and period.

Prepare the review file

  • Operative agreement and fee triggers
  • Evidence of the leasing work
  • Documented fee calculation
  • Credits and prior deductions
  • Approved payment and reporting handoff

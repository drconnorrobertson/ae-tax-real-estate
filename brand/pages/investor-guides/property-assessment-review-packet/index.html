Prepare a property assessment review packet before seeking advice

Organize assessment notices, parcel details, valuation evidence and local process questions for a qualified reviewer.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Property operating guides

An assessment notice, a tax bill and a market-value opinion are different documents. Before asking whether a property assessment should be challenged, assemble the exact notice and facts that a qualified local reviewer needs. This guide organizes the file; it does not identify a filing deadline, promise a reduction or explain the appeal rules for a particular jurisdiction.

Identify the actual parcel and notice

Record the parcel reference, ownership shown, notice date, assessment period and issuing authority. Keep the complete notice rather than a cropped image of the total. Check whether several parcels or separately assessed improvements are involved. Resolve uncertainty about the property identity before comparing figures from different sources.

Separate the amounts being compared

List the assessed figure, tax-bill amount and any other values with their source dates and definitions. Do not assume a purchase price, loan appraisal and assessment measure the same thing. Ask the local professional to explain the relevant basis of comparison and any adjustments required by the jurisdiction’s process.

Build a property-fact file

Collect current descriptions, area measurements, use, condition and documented changes. Identify possible factual discrepancies without declaring them proven. For a fictional notice describing an extra unit, provide the plans or other reliable evidence and ask the reviewer to assess the discrepancy. A disagreement with the total is not a substitute for supported property facts.

Obtain the official process information

Use the issuing authority’s own instructions to identify forms, stages, required evidence and deadlines. Record the source and date checked. Different locations may use different procedures. Have the reviewer confirm what applies before action is taken; a general internet guide or another owner’s past experience does not establish the current process for this parcel.

Record the decision and follow-up

Keep the reviewer’s recommendation, submitted documents, delivery evidence and response with the original notice. Track payment obligations and procedural questions separately with qualified advice. If no action is recommended, retain the reasoning as well. The file should support the decision made, not imply that assembling documents guarantees a lower assessment.

Prepare the review file

  • Complete notice and parcel identification
  • Clearly labeled values and source dates
  • Supported property facts and discrepancies
  • Official local instructions for professional review
  • Decision, submissions and follow-up records

Sources and review scope

These are editorial preparation steps. IRS guidance explains the importance of supporting records and distinguishes types of rental receipts and costs.

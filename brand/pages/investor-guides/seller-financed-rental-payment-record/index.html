Track seller-financed rental sale payments against the agreed schedule

Maintain a payment and exception record for a seller-financed property transaction without calculating tax treatment from cash receipts.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Property operating guides

After a seller-financed sale, the owner needs records of the agreement, scheduled amounts, actual collections and changes. A bank receipt alone may not explain interest, principal, charges or other items. Build a payment record that a qualified adviser can follow. This guide does not determine installment-sale eligibility, gain, recapture, enforcement rights or the proper tax allocation of a payment.

Gather the completed transaction documents

Keep the executed sale and financing documents, closing statement and relevant amendments together. Record the parties, payment address or servicer, and where the operative schedule is maintained. Separate the agreement from an earlier proposal. Ask qualified advisers to resolve inconsistencies before using a schedule for reporting.

Use the authoritative payment schedule

Obtain the schedule or allocation information from the responsible professional or servicer. Label any working calculation as an estimate until reviewed. Do not infer that every payment has the same composition. Keep source dates and versions so later changes can be matched to the period they affect.

Record actual collections and timing

Link each receipt to bank evidence and the scheduled obligation it is intended to address. For a fictional $2,000 scheduled payment with $1,500 received, the $500 difference is an open reconciliation item. It does not establish an agreed reduction or dictate how the amount received is allocated for tax purposes.

Preserve changes and exceptions

Record returned payments, approved changes, additional amounts and servicing adjustments with supporting documents. Do not overwrite the original schedule to hide a difference. Obtain professional advice about missed payments or modifications rather than assuming an accounting entry resolves contractual questions. Keep correspondence with the transaction file.

Prepare the reporting handoff

Provide the original transaction documents, reviewed schedule, annual receipt history and unresolved-item list to the preparer. Include servicing statements and prior reporting records where relevant. Ask for the required tax treatment to be documented separately from this operational record, and retain the explanation for subsequent years.

Prepare the review file

  • Executed sale and financing documents
  • Reviewed payment schedule and versions
  • Bank-linked receipt history
  • Modification and exception records
  • Annual professional reporting handoff

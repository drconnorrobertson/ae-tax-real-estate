Organize a rental lead-disclosure document review packet

Organize construction-date evidence, available reports and disclosure records for qualified review of rental lead-disclosure questions.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 21, 2026

Property operating guides

Organize records before deciding applicability

This index helps an owner assemble documents for a lead-disclosure review. It is not a lead test, inspection, clearance or finding that a property is safe. The EPA describes a federal disclosure rule applying to most pre-1978 housing and lists exceptions with specific conditions. Record the construction-date evidence, rental arrangement and available reports so an appropriate reviewer can assess the actual facts. Do not treat an old property label alone as a complete applicability analysis.

Use the current federal scope carefully

EPA explains that the rule concerns providing specified information about known lead-based paint and hazards before prospective renters become obligated. Its exceptions include certain short leases that cannot be renewed or extended, as well as other categories subject to stated conditions. The owner should consult the full current guidance rather than rely on a shortened list in a worksheet. State or local requirements may also need separate review. This resource does not determine which exception a property meets or imply that an exception removes every other obligation.

Connect each document to the transaction

Index the disclosure version, available report references, the relevant lease or proposed transaction and evidence of delivery or acknowledgment. Keep the original documents intact. Distinguish an owner’s statement that a report does not exist from a verified search of the file. If a later report arrives, flag it for qualified review rather than assuming an older disclosure remains sufficient. The index records what is available and what remains uncertain; it does not draft substitute disclosure language.

Fictional example: a later report is found

A fictional owner preparing a lease review discovers a prior paint report in a former manager’s archive. The owner records the source, date received and the transaction potentially affected. The reviewer is asked what further steps are needed. The file does not interpret the test results, conclude that hazards are absent or instruct a contractor to disturb painted surfaces. Any environmental evaluation, legal applicability decision or required corrective action belongs with the appropriately qualified professional.

Prepare the handoff without certifying the result

Give the reviewer the building-age sources, relevant occupancy and lease facts, all available reports, document versions and the delivery record. Identify missing pages or disputed dates explicitly. Link the reviewer’s response to the open question and retain it separately from the source material. A completed index means the evidence is organized, not that disclosure duties have been satisfied or the property has passed a safety review. Use EPA’s current forms and guidance where applicable rather than treating this administrative index as an official form. Preserve the source and receipt date of each newly discovered report. Those details help the reviewer understand what information was available at each stage without making an unsupported assumption about earlier knowledge or disclosure.

Track rental keys and access devices by custody and return evidence

Track key and access-device custody using aliases and issue or return evidence, without storing entry credentials or deciding access rights.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 21, 2026

Property operating guides

Track custody without publishing access secrets

A key and access-device log identifies which device alias was issued, returned, replaced or reported missing. It is useful during ordinary tenancies as well as management changes. The existing manager-transition guide handles the wider handoff; this resource keeps the individual device history traceable. Do not store working door codes, alarm credentials, key-cut patterns or other information that would allow entry. A custody record is not permission to enter a dwelling or change locks.

Use aliases and supporting references

Assign a device alias and connect it to the property or unit in a controlled record. Identify the authorized recipient according to the applicable agreement and process. Record issue and return evidence with dates, keeping a replacement distinct from the original device. If the record is incomplete, mark the gap instead of assuming that an unsigned handoff occurred. Keep access to the log limited to those who need it, and handle sensitive identity information through the appropriate secure system.

Fictional example: a device is reported lost

A fictional tenant reports that an access fob is missing. The manager records the report against the device alias and refers the matter through the authorized process. A replacement is linked to that history, with evidence of its issue. The log does not decide whether the tenant owes a fee or whether a lock or access system may lawfully be changed. Those questions depend on the relevant agreement, authority and local requirements, which must be reviewed separately.

Separate physical custody from authority

Someone holding a key may not have authority to use it for every purpose. Keep the custody record distinct from entry notices, vendor approvals or other documents governing access. The vendor-authorization guide can support that separate workflow. The Federal Trade Commission recommends limiting information and access to legitimate business needs; apply that principle to the log’s contents and distribution. Do not circulate a complete property access map merely to prove that a device was returned.

Review changes and unresolved items

At a planned review, compare issue and return references, identify devices without clear status and assign follow-up to an authorized person. Preserve the original record when correcting a mistaken assignment. If a manager changes, provide only the necessary inventory through the agreed handoff process and verify receipt. The useful result is a dated history of custody and open questions. It does not certify building security, replace local legal advice or justify unilateral action against a tenant based solely on a spreadsheet entry. If devices serve common areas as well as a unit, describe that scope through a neutral alias. Avoid attaching unnecessary access instructions; the custodian review should remain separate from any operational instructions for entering the property.

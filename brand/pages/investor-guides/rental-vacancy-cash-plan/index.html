Build a rental vacancy cash plan around dated commitments

Separate expected rent interruption, repairs, leasing costs and operating payments in a vacancy scenario.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Property operating guides

A vacancy affects both receipts and spending, and those changes may happen in different weeks. A useful cash plan starts with the unit’s actual payment calendar, a realistic repair scope and clearly labeled assumptions about leasing. The worksheet supports a cash discussion; it is not a valuation, a promise of occupancy or a decision about the tax treatment of vacant property.

Define the unit and planning period

Record the unit, expected vacancy start and review horizon. Identify whether the scenario concerns a known move-out or an uncertain future event. Keep any rent still due from the previous period separate from expected new rent. A single annual vacancy percentage can hide an immediate cash shortage around a large bill.

List commitments that continue

Map loan payments, insurance, utilities, management charges and other known obligations by expected payment date. Separate items whose amount may change during vacancy. Use current statements and agreements rather than treating every prior-month expense as fixed. Ask the responsible professional to confirm assumptions that depend on coverage, contract terms or financing conditions.

Build the turnover work schedule

Identify inspection, repairs, cleaning, marketing and other planned tasks, including approval and payment timing. For a fictional unit needing $2,000 of work before listing, a contractor deposit can create an earlier outflow than the final invoice. Record quotes and estimates distinctly and identify the work that must finish before the next step can begin.

Test leasing timing without hiding uncertainty

Show separate scenarios for the listing date, new lease start and first collected rent. A signed agreement and an assumed leasing date are different evidence. Include any known concessions or collection timing in the scenario. Explain the effect of a delayed repair or a later move-in instead of presenting a single forecast as certain.

Connect the unit to portfolio cash

Compare the dated outflows and inflows with cash available for the property and the wider portfolio. Identify the source of any proposed funding and who approves it. Review actual results against the earlier scenario and retain the changes. Tax reporting and deductibility should be reviewed separately using the property’s facts and records.

Prepare the review file

  • Defined unit and vacancy horizon
  • Dated continuing payment commitments
  • Turnover scope, quotes and dependencies
  • Separate leasing and collection scenarios
  • Funding source and forecast review date

Reconcile rental utility bills across service periods and units

Match meters, accounts, service periods, payments and approved allocations before using utility totals in a property review.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Property operating guides

A utility payment can cover a different period from the date it leaves the bank, and one account may serve more than one unit. A reconciliation should show the source bill, the covered service and any allocation or reimbursement. It supports a review of actual property records rather than assigning tax treatment from a bank description.

Map accounts to the property

Record utility provider, account reference, meter or service location and responsible payer. Keep sensitive account numbers in the approved record system. Identify shared meters or common areas and any changes in account ownership. A property address alone may not show which unit or service the bill actually covers.

Separate service dates and payment dates

Link each bill to its beginning and ending service dates, issue date, due date and payment record. Identify estimated readings, deposits, credits and catch-up bills. Comparing monthly bank withdrawals without that context can make a timing change look like a sudden change in consumption or price.

Document allocations and reimbursements

Where amounts are divided, record the basis, source information and approval. For a fictional $400 common-service bill allocated equally between two units under an approved method, the working allocation is $200 each. That illustration does not establish that equal allocation is permitted or appropriate for another property. Use the actual arrangement and qualified review.

Investigate unusual movements

Compare similar service periods and consider occupancy, weather, repairs, rate changes and billing corrections. Ask for supporting information before attributing a difference to a tenant or a leak. Technical equipment or safety questions should go to qualified providers. Keep the operational explanation separate from an accounting adjustment until the responsible reviewer approves it.

Prepare the accountant’s handoff

Provide bills, payment evidence, allocation schedules and reimbursement records together. The IRS rental-income guidance discusses tenant-paid owner expenses, so identify those arrangements for the preparer rather than netting them away without explanation. Retain unresolved questions and the written reporting decision with the supporting file.

Prepare the review file

  • Meter and account-to-unit map
  • Service-period and payment references
  • Allocation method and approval
  • Credits and reimbursement records
  • Unusual-use questions and accountant handoff

Sources and review scope

These are editorial preparation steps. IRS guidance explains the importance of supporting records and distinguishes types of rental receipts and costs.

  • IRS: supporting business records
  • IRS: rental income and expenses

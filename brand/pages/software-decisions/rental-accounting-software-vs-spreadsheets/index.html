Rental Accounting Software vs Spreadsheets: Records, Review and Handoff

Compare a rental spreadsheet and software workflow by data completeness, corrections, property detail, collaboration and exports.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Rental software comparisons and selection guides

A spreadsheet and a rental-accounting application can both be part of a workable records process. The better fit depends on the owner’s activity, the people reviewing it and the controls around changes. This guide compares workflows, not a tested ranking of products. Software descriptions from Stessa, Landlord Studio and RentRedi provide examples of features to evaluate; no feature count establishes that records are complete or correct.

Describe the current record flow

List financial accounts, rent channels, property-manager reports and documents used each month. In a spreadsheet, identify where information is entered and how totals are checked. In a proposed application, identify feeds, imports and manual entries. A connected bank feed may reduce entry work while still requiring a process for missing transactions, transfers and property assignments.

Make structure and corrections visible

Use consistent identifiers for properties, units and ownership groups. In a spreadsheet, protect formulas and retain versions under an agreed process. In software, ask how changed categories or assignments are tracked and exported. Neither format removes the need to explain an unusual entry. Choose a method that the owner and reviewer can understand after staff, advisers or systems change.

Compare a representative reporting period

For a fictional owner with several units, use rent receipts, repairs, an insurance payment and an expense shared between properties. Ask what must be entered, reviewed and reconciled in each setup. Record the manual steps without claiming that an unperformed demonstration proves savings. Include the accountant’s needs in the exercise, rather than optimizing only the owner’s data-entry process.

Identify useful features and their plan limits

Stessa, Landlord Studio and RentRedi describe receipt, property-record and report functions, with access and limits varying by product or plan. Check the exact subscription being considered. Do not assume that a free version includes every report or that a paid version handles every desired ownership or accounting scenario. Confirm the cost of the whole portfolio and any optional services.

Distinguish reports from accounting decisions

A generated profit-and-loss view or Schedule E-oriented export can help organize information. It does not decide whether the underlying record is complete, establish tax treatment or file the return. A spreadsheet total has the same limitation. Assign responsibility for reconciliation, accounting questions and preparation regardless of which tool supplies the report.

Plan migration and retention before switching

Keep a dated copy of the old records and define the opening position in the new process. Confirm how transaction detail, attachments and corrections remain accessible. Have the owner and reviewer agree when the migration is complete. A tool change should preserve a usable record trail and a clear handoff, rather than merely replace the screen on which incomplete records are stored.

Decision checklist

  • Complete list of financial and rental-data sources
  • Property identifiers and consistent reporting periods
  • Formula or category-change review process
  • Representative month evaluated in both workflows
  • Plan limits and full portfolio cost
  • Migration, exports and document retention plan

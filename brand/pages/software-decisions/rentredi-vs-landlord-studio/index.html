RentRedi vs Landlord Studio: Rent Records and Accounting Handoffs

Compare RentRedi and Landlord Studio by rent-to-ledger workflow, expense review, plan access and report exports.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Rental software comparisons and selection guides

A landlord choosing between RentRedi and Landlord Studio should trace how rent and expenses reach the records used for review. Both describe rental accounting features alongside property-management activity. This comparison uses official documentation and focuses on the handoff, rather than claiming independent product testing. The relevant question is whether the proposed setup covers the owner’s actual properties, payment sources and reporting requirements.

Trace the rental-income source

RentRedi’s accounting FAQ says income in its rent-collection and deposit dashboards flows into the financial views used for profit-and-loss and year-end summaries. Landlord Studio describes online rent payments recorded as income. Ask how each proposed setup handles rent received outside the platform, partial payments, returned payments and manager remittances. A payment dashboard should be reconciled with the relevant account activity, not assumed to contain every receipt.

Review expenses and property assignments

RentRedi describes receipt extraction, expense categories and owner review of amounts, categories and property assignments. Landlord Studio describes receipt capture, bank feeds and suggested matches, with several automated functions on Pro. Ask how duplicate receipts, shared costs and incorrect suggestions are resolved. Keep the supporting document and the person responsible for the decision visible; an automation label is not a substitute for review.

Check access to the needed plan features

RentRedi’s FAQ says the described accounting suite is included for landlord subscribers. That statement does not mean the underlying subscription or every related service is free. Landlord Studio describes Go for up to three units and paid Pro features including bank feeds, accountant access and reports. Confirm the current subscription, unit count and feature access before comparing the full cost.

Define the report handoff

Both describe financial exports or rental reports that may support a preparer’s work. RentRedi explicitly distinguishes its Schedule E summary from filing a tax return. Apply the same practical distinction when reviewing any software output: identify who checks the records, resolves accounting questions and prepares the actual filing. Confirm date filters, property detail and attachment access using a sample export.

Walk through a realistic exception

For a fictional owner, include one rent payment collected in the platform, one received elsewhere and a refund relating to an earlier period. Ask each provider to explain how the entries are captured and how the owner checks for omissions or duplicates. This is a proposed comparison exercise, not an assertion that either product mishandles the example. Record any manual steps the demonstration reveals.

Consider migration and ongoing responsibility

List the history, balances, receipts and tenant records that would need to move. Agree who verifies the opening position and how older records remain accessible. Review export options and the process for providing information to a new accountant. Select a workflow the owner can operate consistently, and treat unsupported assumptions as questions to resolve before migrating.

Decision checklist

  • All rent channels and settlement accounts
  • Process for missing, duplicate or returned payments
  • Property assignment and expense-correction workflow
  • Current subscription and unit-based charges
  • Sample property and portfolio exports
  • Migration owner and opening-balance review

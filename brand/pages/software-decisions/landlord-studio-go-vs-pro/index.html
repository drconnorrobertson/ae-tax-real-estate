Landlord Studio Go vs Pro: Manual Records, Bank Feeds and Reports

Compare Landlord Studio Go and Pro by unit count, data capture, accountant access and the financial reports required.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Rental software comparisons and selection guides

Landlord Studio describes Go as a free way to track rental income and expenses, while Pro adds features such as bank feeds, accounting connections and reports. The decision should reflect the owner’s recordkeeping process and portfolio size. This guide summarizes the provider’s public description and outlines a proposed comparison exercise; it does not claim hands-on testing or establish that software output is ready to file as a tax return.

Count units and define the records

The reviewed accounting page describes Go as free for up to three units. Pro is displayed at $15 month to month, with three units included and $1.20 for each additional unit. Confirm current pricing and how your portfolio is counted. A four-unit building is not necessarily the same pricing input as one property, so keep unit and property records distinct.

Separate manual capture from connected activity

The page presents Go as a starting point for income and expense tracking, while bank feeds and AI-assisted bookkeeping are described as Pro features. Ask how historical spreadsheets enter the system and how a landlord reviews newly suggested transactions. A bank connection may reduce typing but does not prove that property assignments, duplicates or missing items have been resolved.

Identify the reporting and collaboration need

Landlord Studio’s accounting page places Schedule E reports and accountant access among Pro capabilities. Ask your preparer what export is needed and whether direct access would improve the review process. A Schedule E-oriented output organizes records; it does not decide tax treatment, replace the complete return or establish that all property information has been supplied.

Check the working process around receipts

The provider describes receipt capture and mileage records in its rental-accounting workflow. Confirm the exact allowances and functions in the plan offered to you. For a fictional owner who often pays expenses away from the office, test how an image is attached, a property is selected and a correction is made. Avoid assuming a feature is unlimited or included without checking the current plan description.

Compare the owner’s total effort

Use one representative period containing rent, ordinary expenses and a shared cost. Note the manual steps required under each proposed plan and which are supported by bank feeds or exports. Include the time spent checking results, not just entering data. This is a suggested demonstration brief, not measured time savings or an independent accuracy test.

Plan changes and data retention

Confirm how data is retained if the landlord adds units, changes plans or moves to another system. Ask how attachments, transaction detail and reports can be exported. Make a clear handoff plan with the accountant before the first reporting deadline. The appropriate plan is the one that supports a workable process at the current verified cost.

Decision checklist

  • Portfolio counted by units and properties
  • Current manual capture and review tasks
  • Bank-feed and import requirements
  • Required reports and accountant access
  • Receipt and mileage functions confirmed by plan
  • Exports and plan-change handling

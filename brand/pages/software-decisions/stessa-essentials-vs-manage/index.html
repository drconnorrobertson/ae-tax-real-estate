Stessa Essentials vs Manage: Reports and Workflow Requirements

Compare Stessa Essentials and Manage using the current feature table, billing terms and the owner’s actual reporting handoff.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Rental software comparisons and selection guides

Stessa lists Essentials as a free starting plan and Manage as a paid option. The decision should follow a feature the owner will actually use, rather than a general preference for a paid tier. This guide uses the current public plan comparison and proposes a review process. It does not test Stessa’s calculations, certify its reports or recommend a tax treatment for any transaction.

Define what the owner needs from the plan

List the recurring tasks: organizing activity, attaching documents, reviewing a property’s results, supplying a tax-preparation package or signing a document. Separate those tasks from features that sound useful but have no planned use. Stessa’s pricing page states that all plans include unlimited properties, so the comparison is not simply about moving past a small property-count allowance.

Read the reporting distinction carefully

The reviewed table describes a basic tax package in Essentials and adds a Schedule E report in Manage. Ask the preparer which output is useful and what information still needs to be supplied separately. A more specifically named report can improve the handoff, but it does not determine whether entries are complete or whether a cost belongs in a particular tax category.

Check what does not change

The same table lists five smart receipt scans per month for both Essentials and Manage, while unlimited scanning appears in Pro. If the problem is a high receipt volume, Manage may not address that particular need. Write down the actual bottleneck and verify the feature that resolves it. Do not infer a capability simply because the plan is paid or marketed as an upgrade.

Confirm billing and optional services

In the reviewed annual-billing view, Manage is displayed at $12 per month paid annually. Confirm the amount charged, renewal timing and cancellation terms for the selected billing option. Review any optional services separately. A monthly-equivalent display is different from a month-to-month commitment, and a free subscription tier does not establish the pricing of every connected service.

Use a proposed month-end handoff

For a fictional owner with three rentals, assemble a month of transactions, supporting documents and one shared expense. Compare the available exports and the manual information the preparer still requests. If a Manage-only report removes a documented recurring step, record that benefit along with its cost. This is a suggested evaluation method, not a claim that this publication performed the test.

Keep future changes separate from today’s need

If the owner may later need budgeting, broader reports or higher scanning capacity, identify those as future requirements and verify which plan currently covers them. Confirm the ability to export records when changing plans. Choose the present scope based on documented tasks, and revisit the decision when activity or reporting needs change rather than treating an upgrade as permanent necessity.

Decision checklist

  • Actual month-end and tax-preparation tasks
  • Required report fields and date filters
  • Receipt volume compared with plan allowances
  • Selected billing period and renewal terms
  • Optional services priced separately
  • Export and future-plan requirements

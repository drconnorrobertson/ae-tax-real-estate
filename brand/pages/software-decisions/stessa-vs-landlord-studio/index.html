Stessa vs Landlord Studio: Rental Bookkeeping and Plan Fit

Compare Stessa and Landlord Studio by portfolio structure, transaction capture, report access and the work retained by the owner.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Rental software comparisons and selection guides

The comparison between Stessa and Landlord Studio should begin with the records an owner needs to maintain, not a list of every available feature. Both describe rental-focused financial tools. Their free and paid plans use different limits and reporting boundaries. This guide summarizes public information and proposes a matching exercise; it does not establish which product is better for every landlord or imply that either replaces an accountant.

Count the right portfolio items

Stessa’s pricing page says all plans include unlimited properties. Landlord Studio’s accounting page describes a free Go plan for up to three units and a Pro plan with three units included, plus additional-unit charges. A property and a unit are different planning measures. List buildings, units and ownership groups before comparing prices so the portfolio is represented consistently.

Compare the capture and review process

Landlord Studio describes bank feeds and suggested matches on Pro, as well as receipt capture, mileage records and property or unit tracking. Stessa distinguishes receipt-scanning allowances among its plans and supports financial-account and property-manager information. Ask how the owner reviews imported activity, handles a missing entry and corrects an assignment. Do not assume that connecting a feed completes reconciliation of the account.

Match reports to the preparation handoff

The reviewed Stessa table places a Schedule E report in Manage and additional reports in Pro. Landlord Studio describes Schedule E reporting and accountant access among its Pro features. Identify what your preparer needs and request an example export. A software report may help organize information, but it does not establish the tax treatment of a repair, improvement, payment or ownership arrangement.

Compare the actual portfolio price

Stessa’s reviewed annual-billing view displays Manage at $12 monthly equivalent and Pro at $28 monthly equivalent. Landlord Studio’s page displays Pro at $15 month to month, with three units included and $1.20 for each extra unit. Confirm current billing, unit definitions and optional services before calculating a total. These public figures should not be treated as permanently available personalized quotes.

Evaluate a month that contains exceptions

For a fictional owner with two buildings and four units, include ordinary rent, an insurance payment covering several months and a shared maintenance bill. Ask how each system represents the units, assigns the records and exports the result. Have the accountant identify anything that needs additional review. This exercise tests fit for a real workflow without claiming that we operated or validated either product.

Preserve a usable audit trail

Ask how changes are recorded and how the owner retrieves attachments, categories and prior reports. Confirm access arrangements when a property manager or accountant changes. Plan how to retain important documents independently where needed. A useful system makes the handoff understandable; it does not remove the owner’s responsibility to supply complete records or the professional’s responsibility to review them.

Decision checklist

  • Buildings, units and ownership groups counted consistently
  • Accounts, manager statements and receipt sources
  • Plan-specific bank feeds and report requirements
  • Four-unit or larger-portfolio price calculation confirmed
  • Representative exceptions reviewed in a demonstration
  • Export and accountant-access arrangements

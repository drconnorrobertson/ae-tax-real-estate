Rental unpaid-bills roll-forward calculator

Reconcile an opening unpaid balance, new bills, credits and payments for one consistent period.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Rental decision calculators

This calculator checks the arithmetic of a simple unpaid-bills roll-forward. It starts with the opening unpaid balance, adds new bills and subtracts credits and payments applied to the same set of obligations. The resulting balance should be reconciled to an itemized schedule. It does not decide expense recognition, deductibility, payment authorization or whether a disputed bill is valid.

Starting values are fictional. Use matching periods and comparable records.

Use one period and one population of bills

Define the period start and end and the properties or entities included. Keep bills for another entity outside the schedule unless you deliberately include and identify them. The opening amount should agree with the prior period’s closing balance or have a documented explanation. A total from one report cannot be reconciled reliably with transactions from a different date range.

Match credits and payments to the obligation

Include only reductions applied to the bills represented in the opening balance or new-bill total. A payment made during the period may relate to a different scope, and a supplier credit may not yet have been applied. Keep the underlying references so those differences can be resolved. The calculator rejects reductions larger than the available balance rather than inventing a negative payable.

Investigate differences instead of forcing agreement

Compare the calculated closing amount with the itemized unpaid list. A missing bill, duplicate entry, unapplied payment or timing difference may explain a mismatch. Do not enter an unexplained credit simply to make the total agree. Record the question and the person responsible for resolving it, then preserve the correction and supporting documentation when the issue is settled.

Keep the roll-forward connected to cash planning

The unpaid total does not show when each amount will be paid. Use the individual due dates and approved payment plan for a separate cash schedule. Also distinguish a recorded bill from work that is expected but not yet billed. Bring the reconciled list and unresolved items to the accounting review without assuming the roll-forward determines how any item belongs in the books or a tax return.

Worked example

Opening unpaid bills of $4,000 plus $9,000 of new bills give $13,000 to reconcile. Subtracting $500 of applied credits and $8,000 of applied payments leaves a $4,500 closing unpaid balance.

What this model leaves out

Unbilled commitments, foreign currency, interest, disputed liability, payment timing and accounting or tax classification need their own supporting review.

Keep the supporting records

Save the input values, period definitions and documents behind the scenario. The IRS business-record guidance describes supporting business records; it does not validate this planning model or determine the treatment of its result.

Annual rent-step schedule calculator

Compare a simple annual percentage rent schedule with unchanged starting rent.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Rental decision calculators

Use this calculator to inspect a specific rent schedule, not to decide what increase is lawful, collectible or appropriate for a tenant. The model starts with one monthly amount, holds it constant for the first twelve months and applies the same percentage at each later year boundary. It compares the scheduled total with keeping the starting monthly amount unchanged for the same number of years.

Starting values are fictional. Use matching periods and comparable records.

Define the start and each increase boundary

The first modeled year uses the starting rent without an immediate increase. Year two uses the starting rent multiplied by one plus the entered percentage. Each subsequent year repeats that multiplication. This convention matters: applying an increase on the first day would produce a different schedule. Check the actual agreement and relevant requirements separately before describing any calculated amount as due.

Keep full years and consistent units

Enter monthly rent, an annual percentage and a whole number of twelve-month years. The tool does not handle a partial first year, different anniversary dates or a percentage that changes between years. Model those situations in a dated schedule instead of rounding a partial period into a full year. All displayed amounts use the same currency assumption; there is no currency conversion.

Distinguish scheduled rent from cash received

The result adds the scheduled amounts. It does not reduce them for vacancy, concessions, nonpayment, expenses or collection timing. A schedule can therefore rise while available cash does not. Use actual receipts and the supporting lease records for a separate cash review. The comparison with unchanged rent is simply a mathematical baseline, not a prediction of what another tenant or agreement would produce.

Check the rounding convention

Calculations retain precision internally and round only the displayed result to cents. An agreement that rounds each annual step before calculating the next step may produce a slightly different total. Identify that difference before using the output in a formal schedule. Preserve the input values and the calculation date with your working file so another reviewer can reproduce the same model.

Worked example

With $1,500 starting monthly rent, a 3% increase after each full year and three modeled years, annual scheduled rent is $18,000, $18,540 and $19,096.20. The total is $55,636.20, which is $1,636.20 above three years at the unchanged starting rent.

What this model leaves out

Partial periods, differing annual percentages, agreement-specific rounding, legal limits, vacancy, concessions, collection and tax treatment are outside this model.

Keep the supporting records

Save the input values, period definitions and documents behind the scenario. The IRS business-record guidance describes supporting business records; it does not validate this planning model or determine the treatment of its result.

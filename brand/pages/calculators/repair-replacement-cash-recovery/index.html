Repair and replacement simple cash-recovery calculator

Explore repair and replacement simple cash-recovery calculator using your own inputs, a transparent calculation and a worked example.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Rental decision calculators

This calculator compares the additional upfront cash for replacement with a stated annual operating saving relative to repair. It reports a simple recovery period. The result is narrow: it does not assess equipment safety, diagnose condition or establish the economically preferred choice. Those questions depend on facts that are not entered into this form.

The starting values are fictional examples. Enter matching periods and comparable costs for your scenario.

Specify both alternatives

Enter the replacement cash cost, repair cash cost and positive annual operating savings expected from replacement relative to repair. Use comparable scopes, including the same installation and removal assumptions where relevant. Savings should describe the difference between the two alternatives, not the entire operating cost of one option. Record whether the amount comes from measured history, a written estimate or an unverified planning assumption. The form does not validate the estimate.

Read the recovery calculation

A $5,000 replacement compared with a $1,000 repair requires $4,000 more upfront. Dividing $4,000 by $500 in annual operating savings yields eight years. The model assumes that annual difference persists without change. If replacement costs no more than repair, the additional-cost recovery line is zero; that convention avoids a negative recovery period. It does not mean replacement has no costs, risks or operational constraints.

Keep omitted differences separate

Useful life, failures, downtime, warranties, financing, disposal proceeds, maintenance timing, tax treatment and the time value of money are excluded. A replacement may have a different capacity or service level, which also needs explicit comparison. The form cannot determine whether repair is technically possible or safe. A specialist assessment and the terms of written proposals remain independent inputs to the decision. When annual savings are uncertain, save several clearly labeled runs rather than presenting one result as assured. The recovery period is not a guarantee that savings will be realized.

Worked example

A $4,000 additional upfront cost divided by $500 of annual operating savings gives a simple eight-year recovery period.

What this model leaves out

No safety, technical condition, warranty, useful-life, financing or tax recommendation.

Before using the result

  • Obtain comparable written scopes for repair and replacement.
  • Show the basis for annual incremental savings.
  • List downtime and warranty differences separately.
  • Keep safety and condition assessments outside the arithmetic.
  • Date each estimate and preserve alternative scenarios.

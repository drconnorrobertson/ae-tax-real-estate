Rental project three-period cash calculator

Show receipt and payment timing across three periods and identify the lowest modeled balance.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Rental decision calculators

Use three consistent periods to inspect the cash timing of a defined rental project. Each period adds the entered receipts and subtracts payments from the preceding balance. The tool identifies the lowest opening or period-end amount and the additional opening cash needed to keep those points nonnegative. It is a conditional arithmetic scenario, not a forecast or a financing recommendation.

Starting values are fictional. Use matching periods and comparable records.

Define what a period represents

Choose weeks, months or identified project stages and use that definition consistently. Record the actual dates separately. The calculator does not infer a date from a period label. A receipt expected after a payment deadline may not support that payment even when both appear in one monthly column. Use a more detailed schedule where the sequence inside a period matters.

Include only the opening cash available to the scenario

Identify which accounts and funds the opening amount represents. Do not treat money restricted to another purpose as freely available without an appropriate review. If the cash is already included in a receipt row, counting it again in the opening balance will overstate resources. Keep the account reconciliation and the project’s funding assumptions with the model so another reviewer can follow them.

Separate commitments from estimates

Label expected receipts and payments according to their supporting evidence. A proposed contribution, unsigned quote and approved payment can carry different uncertainty even when their entered amounts are equal. Create alternative scenarios when timing changes rather than hiding the uncertainty in a single total. The tool does not establish that a payer will provide funds or that a provider can complete the work on schedule.

Interpret the low point carefully

The minimum covers only the opening amount and the three period-end balances. Cash can fall lower inside a period, and obligations outside the chosen scope are omitted. The reported extra opening cash is the arithmetic amount that offsets a negative modeled low point. It does not identify an available funding source, approve borrowing or set an adequate reserve. Review the underlying dates before making a commitment.

Worked example

Start with $10,000. Period 1 adds $5,000 and pays $7,000, ending at $8,000. Period 2 receives zero and pays $12,000, ending at negative $4,000. Period 3 adds $15,000 and pays $5,000, ending at $6,000. An extra $4,000 at the start would bring the lowest modeled period-end balance to zero.

What this model leaves out

Within-period timing, omitted obligations, restricted funds, financing terms, contingency reserves and the certainty of receipts or project completion require separate review.

Keep the supporting records

Save the input values, period definitions and documents behind the scenario. The IRS business-record guidance describes supporting business records; it does not validate this planning model or determine the treatment of its result.

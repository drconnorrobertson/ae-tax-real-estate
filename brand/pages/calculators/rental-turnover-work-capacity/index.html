Rental turnover work-capacity calculator

Compare a defined turnover workload with available person-hours, without treating it as a promised completion date.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Rental decision calculators

This calculator converts a defined group of turnover tasks into person-hours and compares that workload with a weekly capacity assumption. Person-hours measure labor effort, not elapsed clock time. The result can reveal a capacity gap, but it does not schedule crews, verify work quality or promise that a property will be ready for occupancy on a particular date.

Starting values are fictional. Use matching periods and comparable records.

Define the tasks included in one unit

List the tasks represented by the per-unit estimate and use the same scope across the units being modeled. A cleaning-only estimate should not silently include repairs, inspection or supply purchasing. If unit conditions differ materially, model separate groups or build a task-level schedule. Keep estimates distinguishable from actual hours recorded after the work so later comparisons are meaningful.

Use person-hours consistently

Two people working for three hours contribute six person-hours. Enter their combined useful capacity using the same definition. Do not multiply crew size twice by entering crew-hours as if they were person-hours and then also multiplying capacity. Add travel and coordination effort separately when it is not already included in the unit estimate. Explain who is included in the available-hours figure.

Separate effort from sequencing constraints

Work can require access, materials, drying time, specialist availability or completion of another task first. The calculator does not model those dependencies. Rounding workload up to whole weeks at the entered capacity is only a rough capacity comparison. A real completion plan needs dates and task relationships, especially when a deadline depends on work performed by more than one provider.

Review the estimate after actual work

Compare actual task hours with the estimate and record significant reasons for a difference. Avoid using one unusually simple or difficult unit as an unexplained standard for all future turnovers. Update the estimate when scope changes, while retaining the original version used for the decision. Capacity planning should support an honest schedule discussion rather than pressure a team to certify unfinished work.

Worked example

Four units at six person-hours each require 24hours. Adding four coordination and travel hours creates a 28-person-hour workload. At 20 combined person-hours per week, that is 1.4 weeks at the entered capacity, rounded up to two whole weeks at the entered capacity for this coarse comparison.

What this model leaves out

Dependencies, site access, specialist qualifications, safety requirements, material availability, inspection, quality assurance and actual completion dates require separate planning.

Keep the supporting records

Save the input values, period definitions and documents behind the scenario. The IRS business-record guidance describes supporting business records; it does not validate this planning model or determine the treatment of its result.

Percentage versus flat rental management fee

Explore percentage versus flat rental management fee using your own inputs, a transparent calculation and a worked example.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Rental decision calculators

A percentage management fee and a flat monthly fee can be compared arithmetically only after the fee base is clear. This calculator models annual collections using scheduled monthly rent and a collection percentage, then applies the stated management rate. It places that result beside twelve flat monthly payments. Service quality and the rest of the agreement remain separate questions.

The starting values are fictional examples. Enter matching periods and comparable costs for your scenario.

Define the percentage base

Enter total scheduled monthly rent for the units included, the share modeled as collected, the percentage fee and the flat monthly alternative. Collection percentage must fall between zero and 100. This worksheet assumes the percentage fee applies to the modeled collected amount. If a proposal instead charges on scheduled rent, gross receipts including other items, or a minimum fee, its terms are not reproduced automatically. Note those differences before relying on the output.

Interpret the difference

Scheduled rent of $5,000 per month at 90% collection produces modeled annual collections of $54,000. An 8% fee is $4,320. A flat $350 monthly fee totals $4,200 for twelve months. The reported percentage-minus-flat difference is $120 in this example. A positive difference means the modeled percentage fee is higher; a negative difference means it is lower. The sign describes these fee inputs, not which manager offers the better overall engagement.

Complete the comparison outside the form

Leasing fees, renewal charges, repair markups, minimums, taxes and changes in performance are excluded. A flat fee that continues during vacancy may differ materially from an arrangement charged only during occupancy. The form assumes twelve flat payments, so a different charging schedule needs a separate calculation. Ask each proposal to identify the same services and fee triggers. Save a separate scenario when collections change rather than silently replacing the original assumption; this makes the sensitivity of the percentage option visible.

Worked example

Modeled annual collections of $54,000 produce a $4,320 fee at 8%, compared with $4,200 for twelve $350 flat fees.

What this model leaves out

Leasing fees, renewals, repair markups, minimums, taxes and performance differences.

Before using the result

  • Confirm whether the rate applies to collections or scheduled rent.
  • List minimum fees and vacancy provisions.
  • Separate leasing and renewal charges from ongoing management.
  • Compare the same property group and service scope.
  • Keep the collection assumption with the saved result.

Rent increase and vacancy break-even calculator

Explore rent increase and vacancy break-even calculator using your own inputs, a transparent calculation and a worked example.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Rental decision calculators

An advertised rent increase does not immediately become additional cash when a unit sits empty. This calculator puts the proposed monthly increase beside a simple initial gap: rent forgone during the modeled vacancy plus a separate turnover amount. It then shows how many occupied months of the increase would equal that gap. The result is a comparison worksheet, not a prediction that a tenant will stay for that period.

The starting values are fictional examples. Enter matching periods and comparable costs for your scenario.

Use comparable amounts

Enter the current monthly rent and the proposed monthly rent for the same unit and the same included services. The proposed figure must exceed the current figure. Vacancy can be fractional: half a month is 0.5. Enter turnover cash costs separately, keeping an itemized note of what you included. Avoid putting forgone rent into the turnover field as well, because the model already counts it through the vacancy input.

Read the recovery period

The initial gap uses current rent multiplied by vacancy months, then adds turnover costs. Dividing that amount by the monthly rent increase gives occupied months to recover it. With current rent of $1,500, proposed rent of $1,600, one month vacant and $500 of turnover costs, the gap is $2,000. A $100 monthly increase equals that gap after 20 occupied months. Those are occupied months following vacancy, rather than months from today.

Keep the scenario boundaries visible

This arithmetic holds the rent difference constant. It does not model financing, taxes, discounting, later vacancies, changes in collection performance or another turnover. A longer vacancy changes the initial gap immediately. A smaller achievable increase changes the recovery period substantially. Save separate runs for the alternatives you are actually comparing, with the input date and the source of the rent estimate. A zero gap produces zero recovery months, but that does not establish that changing tenants has no other consequences.

Worked example

$2,000 gap / $100 monthly uplift =20 occupied months.

What this model leaves out

Financing, tax, time value of money, collection changes and later turnover.

Before using the result

  • Document which utilities and services each rent includes.
  • Separate vacancy rent from invoices for turnover work.
  • Label fractional vacancy assumptions clearly.
  • Compare the modeled occupied period with the period under discussion.
  • Retain exclusions beside the result when sharing it.

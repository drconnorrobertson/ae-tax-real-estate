Annual vendor contract versus per-visit calculator

Explore annual vendor contract versus per-visit calculator using your own inputs, a transparent calculation and a worked example.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Rental decision calculators

An annual service contract can be compared with paying per visit when both options cover the same work. This calculator totals a chosen number of visits plus a stated annual extras amount, compares that with the contract price and identifies a whole-visit threshold. It cannot determine whether the services, response commitments or exclusions are equivalent.

The starting values are fictional examples. Enter matching periods and comparable costs for your scenario.

Make the scope comparable

Enter the annual contract price, the per-visit price, a whole number of expected visits and annual extras for the per-visit scenario. The visit price must be positive. Extras are treated as a fixed annual amount in this model, not an amount added to every visit. If there is a charge per visit, include it consistently in the per-visit price or calculate an appropriate annual total. Do not include the same expense in both fields.

Understand the threshold

At $100 per visit, ten visits and $100 of annual extras produce a $1,100 per-visit scenario. A $1,200 contract is $100 higher at that usage. With the same extras, eleven visits reach $1,200, so eleven is the minimum whole-visit count at which the contract is no more expensive. The threshold rounds upward when the arithmetic produces a fraction. If extras alone reach the contract price, the threshold is zero under the entered assumptions.

Separate price from service commitments

The form treats visit scope and annual extras as constant. Emergency surcharges, caps on included visits, material charges, cancellation provisions and response-time differences are outside the calculation. A contract with unlimited routine calls may still exclude particular work. A per-visit proposal may offer a different appointment window. Record these differences beside the result before describing either option as cheaper overall. The expected visit count is a scenario input, not a forecast supplied by the calculator. Historical visits can inform that assumption but do not guarantee future demand.

Worked example

10visits +$100extras =$1,100; contract reaches parity at11visits.

What this model leaves out

Contract-scope differences, caps, emergency rates and variable extra fees must be checked separately.

Before using the result

  • Compare included tasks and covered equipment.
  • Clarify whether extras are annual or per visit.
  • List emergency charges and included-visit caps.
  • Record cancellation and renewal provisions separately.
  • Keep the assumed visit count attached to the comparison.

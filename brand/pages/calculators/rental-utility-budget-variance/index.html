Rental utility budget variance calculator

Explore rental utility budget variance calculator using your own inputs, a transparent calculation and a worked example.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Rental decision calculators

A utility bill above budget can be expressed as a dollar difference or a percentage of the budget. This calculator shows both for one matched monthly comparison period, plus a simple twelve-period scenario using the same dollar difference. The annualized line is deliberately mechanical: it is not a forecast adjusted for weather, occupancy, billing days or utility rates.

The starting values are fictional examples. Enter matching periods and comparable costs for your scenario.

Match the periods first

Enter the budget amount and actual amount for the same monthly period and the same group of utility services. The budget must be positive because it forms the denominator of the percentage calculation. Actual cost may be zero. A monthly budget compared with a bill covering six weeks does not isolate overspending. Likewise, combining electricity and water in one figure but only electricity in the other makes the percentage difficult to interpret.

Read dollars and percentages together

If budget is $200 and actual cost is $250, actual minus budget is $50. Dividing $50 by the $200 budget gives 25%. Repeating that exact $50 difference twelve times gives $600. A negative difference means actual cost is below the stated budget. It does not by itself identify improved efficiency: an estimated reading, an unoccupied period or a shifted invoice date may also change the result.

Use the annualized line as a scenario

Multiplication by twelve answers what would happen if the same difference repeated twelve times. It does not establish that the difference will recur. Seasonal heating and cooling can make a single month unrepresentative. Keep the source bill, service dates, meter notes and budget assumptions available when reviewing the result. The form does not separate usage from unit price, fixed charges, adjustments or arrears. Those components require a bill-level comparison. A useful follow-up worksheet can place several matched periods side by side without treating one unusual bill as a full-year pattern.

Worked example

$50 above budget is25%; repeating it12times gives$600.

What this model leaves out

Annualized difference is a what-if scenario, not a weather-adjusted forecast.

Before using the result

  • Align service dates and billing periods.
  • Use the same utilities in both input amounts.
  • Identify estimated readings and account adjustments.
  • Label the twelve-period output as a scenario.
  • Retain bill details to distinguish usage from rate changes.

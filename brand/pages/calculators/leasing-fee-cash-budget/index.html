Leasing-fee cash budget calculator

Model a quoted percentage fee and flat charge using a clearly defined rent base.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Rental decision calculators

Use this calculator when a proposal defines a fee as a percentage of scheduled rent plus a flat charge. It makes that arithmetic visible and spreads the total across the modeled lease months for comparison. The spread is a planning measure, not an accounting amortization rule. Confirm the proposal’s actual fee base, payment event and exclusions before relying on this simplified structure.

Starting values are fictional. Use matching periods and comparable records.

Confirm what the percentage applies to

The model multiplies monthly rent by the entered number of months and then applies the quoted percentage to that total. Some proposals use a different base, such as rent actually collected, a first-month amount or a minimum charge. Those structures require a different calculation. Do not enter a percentage from one proposal while using the fee base defined in another.

Separate the flat charge from other services

Enter only the additional flat amount you intend to include in this comparison. Keep photography, advertising, screening, renewal or other charges identifiable in the underlying quote. A service label does not establish that it is included in the percentage. Ask the provider to explain the written scope, any minimum and the events that trigger an extra charge.

Keep cash timing outside the monthly spread

Dividing the fee by lease months does not mean the provider accepts monthly payments. The entire charge may be due at a particular event under the actual agreement. Put the real payment timing in your cash calendar. A low monthly equivalent can still accompany a large upfront payment, and the calculator should not be used to hide that difference.

Compare proposals using the same scenario

Use matching rent, term and included services when comparing outputs. For each proposal, retain the source date and explain any adjustment made to create a comparable scenario. The number alone does not measure service quality or establish that a provider is qualified. Keep cancellation, replacement-tenant and renewal questions in a separate written review of the agreement.

Worked example

At $1,500 monthly rent for twelve months, the modeled fee base is $18,000. A 5% charge is $900; adding a $100 flat amount gives a $1,000 total, or about $83.33 per modeled lease month. Actual payment timing follows the agreement.

What this model leaves out

Minimums, fee caps, differing rent bases, renewals, cancellation provisions, other services, payment timing and accounting or tax treatment are not determined here.

Keep the supporting records

Save the input values, period definitions and documents behind the scenario. The IRS business-record guidance describes supporting business records; it does not validate this planning model or determine the treatment of its result.

Rental project allowance and contingency calculator

Show the budget effect of selected alternates, owner allowances and a stated contingency percentage.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Rental decision calculators

Use this calculator to make a simple project budget visible before comparing it with available cash. It adds a base proposal, selected alternates and additional allowances, then applies the entered contingency percentage to the whole subtotal. It does not determine a suitable contingency, certify the completeness of a quote or establish a construction contract price.

Starting values are fictional. Use matching periods and comparable records.

Check for duplicated allowances

Read the base proposal before adding an owner allowance. If the provider already included an amount for the same work or material, adding it again will overstate the scenario. Keep the included base scope and each additional allowance in a short schedule. An allowance is an estimate for identified work, not evidence that the work has a fixed price or an approved final specification.

Select alternates deliberately

Include only the optional items management intends to model. Label options still being considered instead of treating them all as approved commitments. If choosing one alternate replaces part of the base scope, this additive model may not fit until the base amount is adjusted appropriately. Preserve the provider’s written explanation so the arithmetic reflects the intended combination rather than a collection of unrelated totals.

Make the contingency base explicit

The percentage applies to base amount plus selected alternates plus additional allowances. It is a planning reserve in this model and does not change the provider’s contractual entitlement. Another budgeting policy may apply contingency to a narrower base or use separate reserves for different risks. Do not compare percentages alone without checking which amounts each percentage applies to.

Keep commitments and available funds separate

Compare the combined planning amount with a dated cash schedule and identify which costs are already committed. Adding a contingency does not make uncertain work fully defined or ensure that funding is available when payments fall due. Update the schedule when actual quotes or approved changes replace estimates. Keep the earlier version so the owner can see why the budget changed.

Worked example

A $20,000 base proposal plus $1,500 of selected alternates and $2,000 of additional allowances gives a $23,500 subtotal. A 10% contingency on that subtotal is $2,350, producing a $25,850 combined planning amount.

What this model leaves out

Quote completeness, contract interpretation, technical scope, taxes, financing, payment dates and the appropriate reserve level are not determined by this arithmetic.

Keep the supporting records

Save the input values, period definitions and documents behind the scenario. The IRS business-record guidance describes supporting business records; it does not validate this planning model or determine the treatment of its result.

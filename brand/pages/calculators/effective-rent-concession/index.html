Effective rent after an upfront concession

Explore effective rent after an upfront concession using your own inputs, a transparent calculation and a worked example.

AE Tax Advisors · Published November 20, 2026

Rental decision calculators

A concession can make two lease offers with different advertised rents difficult to compare. This calculator spreads a stated upfront rent credit across the modeled lease term to show an effective monthly amount. It is a simple average of scheduled rent after the concession. It does not rewrite the lease, move payment due dates or describe when income is recognized.

The starting values are fictional examples. Enter matching periods and comparable costs for your scenario.

Build the term total

Enter the monthly scheduled rent, the number of months in the comparison term and the total concession amount applied to that term. The term must be positive, and the credit cannot exceed scheduled rent for the term. Use the total credit rather than the number of free months. For example, if one month at $1,800 is credited, enter $1,800 in the credit field. Check whether the actual offer includes conditions or additional charges before treating that credit as comparable.

Distinguish the average from the bill

At $1,800 per month for 12 months, scheduled rent totals $21,600. Subtracting a $1,800 concession leaves $19,800, or $1,650 per modeled month. The monthly bill may still be $1,800 in most months. The effective figure is useful for describing the term as a whole; it is not a promise that each payment equals the average. A credit issued only after a requirement is met should remain visibly conditional in the comparison.

Compare the same package

The calculator excludes deposits, utilities, application costs, parking and other charges unless they are deliberately represented in the rent or credit figures. Mixing an inclusive offer with an offer that charges those items separately can obscure the difference. It also ignores payment timing and the time value of money. Keep the lease schedule next to the result, particularly when comparing different term lengths. The model cannot determine enforceability, eligibility for a concession or tax treatment.

Worked example

($21,600 - $1,800) /12 =$1,650.

What this model leaves out

The result does not change the lease payment schedule or determine tax recognition.

Before using the result

  • Record the credit amount and any conditions.
  • Use the same comparison period across offers.
  • Keep refundable deposits outside rent credits.
  • List recurring charges excluded from the inputs.
  • Preserve the actual payment schedule alongside the average.
